# Commercial Experiments and Falsification Plan

The purpose of Mercatus is not to confirm Echelon's preferred story. It is to discover which commercial claims survive contact with buyers.

## Experiment family A: problem versus framework language

For each Clarity, EDF, and HelixNote wedge, create paired messaging:
A. problem-first, framework name absent or secondary;
B. framework/product-first.

Measure meaningful reply, conversation, problem recognition, internal forwarding, qualification, and paid conversion. Impressions alone do not decide the experiment.

## Experiment family B: diagnosis willingness to pay

Test bounded diagnostics separately:
- Recurring Failure Diagnostic;
- Before You Rewrite Diagnostic;
- AI Value Diagnostic;
- AI System Diagnostic;
- Engineering Throughput Diagnostic;
- Technical Second Opinion;
- Evidence Integrity Review.

For each, test whether prospects will:
1. discuss the problem;
2. supply evidence;
3. accept bounded scope;
4. pay;
5. act on the result;
6. return/refer.

These are distinct gates.

## Experiment family C: independence

Compare:
- independent diagnosis with implementation optional;
- diagnosis bundled toward implementation.

Test trust, willingness to engage, close rate, price tolerance, and implementation follow-through. Do not assume independence wins.

## Experiment family D: proof

For the same message, vary proof level:
- reasoning only;
- worked demonstration;
- internal Echelon case;
- anonymized external case;
- measured external outcome where available.

Measure the minimum proof needed by buyer role and commercial motion.

## Experiment family E: forwardability

Give champions a concise decision/diagnostic asset and observe whether it creates engagement from hidden stakeholders. Compare long report, one-page executive artifact, technical appendix, and role-specific proof.

## Experiment family F: HelixNote jobs-to-be-done

Do not begin with feature preference. Test jobs:
- reconstruct chronology;
- trace claim to evidence;
- find contradiction;
- determine what changed;
- record what was searched for but not found;
- prepare defensible packet;
- resume a complex case after weeks/months;
- turn reviewed conversations into structured case material.

Compare against the user's current workflow.

## Experiment family G: Echelon portfolio comprehension

Test whether prospects understand:
1. Echelon Foundry alone;
2. Echelon + problem-specific diagnostic;
3. named EDF;
4. named Clarity;
5. named HelixNote;
6. combined portfolio story.

Measure explanation time, recall, confusion, perceived relevance, and next action.

## Experiment family H: vertical versus horizontal

Test the same core problem in:
- software/engineering;
- AI investment/governance;
- investor/diligence;
- foodservice;
- professional evidence/case work;
- accessibility.

Do not generalize success across populations until replicated.

## Experiment family I: trigger timing

For a valid problem, compare outreach when a buying trigger is present versus generic prospecting. Triggers include incident, rewrite proposal, AI budget review, leadership change, acquisition, failed implementation, compliance event, conference, cost mandate, and growth stress.

## Experiment family J: economic scalability

For each paid motion capture:
- acquisition effort;
- founder sales time;
- delivery hours;
- reusable versus bespoke work;
- agent/API cost;
- external cost;
- elapsed time;
- price;
- gross contribution estimate;
- follow-up burden;
- referral/expansion.

A motion can have strong customer value and still fail as an Echelon business model.

# Stop and pivot rules

A commercial hypothesis should be reviewed when:
- problem recognition is repeatedly low;
- recognized problem has no urgency;
- buyers repeatedly choose a substitute;
- willingness to pay is below delivery economics;
- evidence burden makes the diagnostic impractical;
- founder dependence cannot be reduced;
- outcomes cannot be demonstrated;
- buyer population is too costly to reach;
- regulatory/ethical constraints make the motion unattractive;
- another wedge repeatedly performs better.

Review does not automatically mean rejection. It requires an explicit decision and evidence.

# Positive escalation rules

A wedge deserves more investment when multiple independent signals appear:
- unsolicited problem language matches the hypothesis;
- meaningful conversations repeat;
- buyers provide evidence/access;
- paid conversion occurs;
- clients act on results;
- measurable outcomes appear;
- repeat/referral occurs;
- delivery becomes more repeatable;
- acquisition and delivery economics improve.

No single viral post, enthusiastic conversation, or successful engagement is sufficient.

# Required experiment record

Every experiment must retain hypothesis, population, trigger, offering/motion, message, proof, CTA, channel, time window, cost, human effort, agent/model effort, stopping rule, raw outcomes, interpretation, contradictory evidence, decision, and follow-up obligations.
